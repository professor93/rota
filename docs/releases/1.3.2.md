## rota 1.3.2

### Changed for people coming from 1.3.1

- **An open `rota run` keeps its login alive by itself.** 1.3.1 rescues a home whose daemon gave up on its login, or whose store Claude Code blanked, but only when some rota command looks at it: `rota list`, a launch, or `rota serve` maintenance. An interactive `rota run` replaces itself with the CLI, so no rota was left to look, and a window open for days said `Login expired · Please run /login` until somebody happened to run `rota list`. Now every interactive `rota run` on a claude account's stored login starts one watcher before it hands over, and the watcher makes that same look, for that account alone, every five minutes for as long as anything runs in the home. The rescue's rules are the same: the store's lock, Claude Code's refresh lock, a refused token never presented twice. A healthy home is left to Claude Code, and nothing is refreshed outside a rescue.
- **What it adds.** A background process, `rota serve watch <id>`, which is internal and not meant to be typed; `.rota-watch.lock` in the account's home, which keeps it to one watcher per home; and `watch.log` in the store, where it says what it did, moved aside to `watch.log.1` at one MiB. Nothing else changes: the CLI's process, pid, terminal, signals, job control, environment and arguments are what they were in 1.3.1. rota does not become the CLI's parent, and the watcher is not its child.

### What to do

Nothing. An interactive `rota run` on a claude account's own login starts the watcher by itself, and it goes by itself once nothing runs in the home.

### The watcher

- It starts with every interactive `rota run`, `-i` and `--` included, on a claude account whose login lives in its home and whose launch took the stored route. `--share` starts the same watcher. A question, a hermetic run, the HTTP API, an account on a token in its environment, one in a directory you chose, and one on a key or a token alone start none. Windows starts none, and neither does a unix without `getsid`, where it could not leave the session of the run that started it.
- It runs in a session of its own, at `/`, with stdin, stdout and stderr on `/dev/null` and no other descriptor. It starts before the run's claim is kept across the exec, so it never holds the claim: once the CLI has exited the account is free. It belongs to init, not to the CLI.
- It goes when nothing is alive in the home (no daemon, no live session, no run holding the account), when the account is removed, dead, given another home or moved to the environment route, and on SIGTERM or SIGHUP. It never goes in the middle of a look, so a refresh is never cut off before its save. A second watcher of one home goes at once and says nothing, and a store it cannot open is tried again at the next look.
- Beside a running `rota serve` it is one more look: both take the store's lock.
- No watcher has yet been seen rescuing a real home under a live Claude Code. Its rescues are tested against fake homes and a fake token endpoint, and it relies, as 1.3.1 does, on Claude Code reading the login that rota writes into its store.

### For the SDK

- `Store.Watchable` reports whether a watcher has anything to keep for an account, and `store.Watch` is the watcher itself: it takes how to open the store, the account id and where to say things, and `store.WatchEvery` is how often it looks.
